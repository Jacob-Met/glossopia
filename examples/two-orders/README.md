# One market, two ways to speak

Yesterday, you brought home a lantern. Today, the language puts the action earlier in the sentence. Can you finish another exchange without rewriting yesterday's conversation?

Open the GLOSSOPIA 0.6.8 standalone HTML release asset in Chrome, or launch the extracted Node package with `npm start`. The companion **two-orders.glossopia.json** in this directory is the project to open inside the app.

This worked project contains two accepted versions of Luma: version 1 uses speaker → quantity → thing → action (SOV); version 2 uses speaker → action → quantity → thing (SVO). The words and two-pear price stay the same. One completed conversation belongs to version 1; one empty conversation belongs to version 2.

## Try it before reading the answers

1. **Keep your own project first.** Choose **Save a copy** and check that your browser downloaded it. Choose **Open** and select **two-orders.glossopia.json**. A successful Open replaces the project in this tab and clears unsent practice sentences, proposal form text, and any proposal preview. Exported project files do not include sentence or proposal drafts; copy any draft text you want separately. You do not need to propose or accept a change for this exercise. The language strip should show latest accepted version 2 and practice version 2.
2. **Visit yesterday's exchange.** Choose **Versions**. Under **YOUR PRACTICE**, open the conversation labelled **version 1 · Complete**. In **The market**, read its three player statements and check the practice version in the language strip. Find `mi un lum na`. Predict: will those exact words work under version 2? Has opening this old conversation changed the latest accepted version?
3. **Test your prediction.** Choose **Versions**, then **Inspect version 2**. In **Language studio**, find **Meaning you can inspect.** Enter `mi un lum na` and press **Inspect**. Rearrange the same words to make a request under the displayed version 2; press **Inspect** again. Then choose **Versions → Inspect version 1** and test the original sentence there. Inspection explains a sentence; it does not submit it to the trader or move inventory.
4. **Make today's exchange.** Choose **Versions** and, under **YOUR PRACTICE**, reopen the supplied **version 2 · Stage 1** conversation. Use **Say it** in **The market** to request one lantern, give two pears, then confirm that you have the lantern. Use **YOUR WORDS** and the sentence-shape guide if needed. Check the bag after each statement. Finally revisit the completed version 1 conversation: what changed, and what stayed attached to its original version?

You do not need to propose or accept any language change, or start another practice, to complete this exercise. **Save a copy** can keep your resulting project as a separate portable file.

## Answer key — read after trying

| Observation | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Open yesterday's conversation | Practice uses version 1; latest accepted language remains version 2. |
| Inspect `mi un lum na` under version 2 | It does not form a sentence under those rules. Its accepted words are in the old order. |
| Inspect `mi na un lum` under version 2 | One clear meaning: I request one lantern. |
| Inspect `mi un lum na` under version 1 | One clear meaning: I request one lantern. |
| Revisit yesterday after today's exchange | Its recorded statements still use version 1 and its completed inventory remains its own. |

Today's three statements, in order:

1. `mi na un lum` — request one lantern. Your bag remains three pears and no lantern.
2. `mi di du pon` — give two pears. Your bag becomes one pear and one lantern.
3. `mi si un lum` — confirm possession. The conversation completes without another transfer.

The version displayed in the studio and the version used by the active practice are separate. Selecting **Inspect version 1** does not convert a version 2 conversation. Each conversation keeps the accepted language version it started with.
